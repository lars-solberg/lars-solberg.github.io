THE SECURITY MODEL HAS CHANGED

Familiar threats. Different control points.

Kubernetes changes where we enforce security, and how fast things move.

The IP isn't the identity

Pods come and go. Their IPs get reused.

Your firewall may never see the traffic

Two workloads can communicate without passing through your perimeter firewall.

You delete it. It comes back.

A controller can restart the workload you just stopped.

Separate containers. Shared kernel.

Workloads share a host kernel. Runtime restrictions and network policies keep them apart.

The rest of the map

Every box is a control point.

Ingress / Gateway API: an F5 or NetScaler built into the cluster. TLS, routing, auth, rate limits and WAF, per route.

Service mesh: an identity on every call, mTLS between pods.

cert-manager: who may mint a certificate for your domain?

Egress gateway: selected pods leave through a fixed IP. Your firewall can tell which app it is.

Operators: Kubernetes as an engine. Define your own objects, and get the API, RBAC and audit log for free.

The walls: os-namespaces, cgroups, seccomp, AppArmor/SELinux, run policies.

EDR, firewalls, hardening: they see nodes and IPs, not pods and identities.
